Why your brain needs a full night of sleep

Sleep can look like the brain switching off, but brain scans tell a different story. During the night the brain moves through cycles of about 90 minutes. Each cycle has lighter sleep, deep slow-wave sleep and REM sleep, the stage in which most vivid dreams happen.

Deep sleep comes mostly early in the night. In this stage the brain replays what you learned during the day and moves important memories into long-term storage. Studies in which people learned word lists before bed found that they remembered more the next morning than people who stayed awake for the same number of hours.

REM sleep becomes longer towards the morning. It seems to help with emotions and with linking ideas in new ways, which may be why a problem can feel easier after a night's rest.

Sleep also has a cleaning job. While you sleep, the fluid around brain cells flows faster and carries away waste that builds up during the day.

Cutting a night short removes mostly the last cycles, and with them a large share of REM sleep. Most adults need seven to nine hours. Simple habits help: a regular bedtime, a dark and cool room, and no bright screens in the last hour before sleep.
